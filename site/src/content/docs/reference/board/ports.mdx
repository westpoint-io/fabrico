A port is a spot on a machine's edge where a belt or a wire connects. Each port has a role, which says whether things go in or come out, a medium, which says whether it takes a belt or a wire, and a side, which says which edge of the machine it faces. The page for each machine lists its ports with all three.

In the example below, a Source sends items into a Router's input on its west side. The Router sends them out of two outputs on its east side, and a belt waits on its Fault port on the south side. Underneath, a Route and an HTTP Handler are joined by wire ports, and a request goes over and its reply comes back.

## Roles

| Role | What it does |
|---|---|
| <Fact id="port.role.In" /> | Takes items or requests into the machine. |
| <Fact id="port.role.Out" /> | Sends items or requests out of the machine. A machine with more than one output numbers them, and a Router's routes pick an output by its number. |
| <Fact id="port.role.Fault" /> | Sends the machine's faults out, apart from its normal outputs. |

A belt runs from an output to an input. Its tail docks on an output or a Fault port, and its head docks on an input. A wire also runs from an output to an input.

## The Fault port

When a machine can't handle an item, the fault leaves by its Fault port, never with the normal items. Dock a belt there and lead it to a Debug Sink to keep each fault where you can read it. With nothing docked, the fault goes no further and the machine shows as faulted. A Route and a Tee have no Fault port on the grid. In the example above, the belts on the Router's and the Handler's Fault ports stay empty, because nothing faults. The [Fault reasons](../fault-reasons/) page says which machine gives each reason and where its fault goes.

## Media

| Medium | Takes | Found on |
|---|---|---|
| <Fact id="port.medium.Mechanical" /> | A belt | Every port of the Source, Router, Tee, Balancer, Merge, Factory and Sink, and the HTTP Handler's Fault port |
| <Fact id="port.medium.Electrical" /> | A wire | The input and output of a Route, and the input and output of an HTTP Handler |

The app refuses a belt docked on a wire port, or a wire on a belt port. [How to fix](../../../troubleshooting/refusals/#refusal-medium-mismatch)

## Sides

| Side | Edge of the machine |
|---|---|
| <Fact id="port.side.N" /> | North, the top edge |
| <Fact id="port.side.E" /> | East, the right edge |
| <Fact id="port.side.S" /> | South, the bottom edge |
| <Fact id="port.side.W" /> | West, the left edge |

A port faces the cell just outside its side, and a belt docks on it only when the belt's end sits on that cell. The app refuses a dock anywhere else. [How to fix](../../../troubleshooting/refusals/#refusal-invalid-dock)

The sides on each machine page are for the machine before any turn. Turning a machine turns its ports with it, so a port on the west side faces north after one turn clockwise. The [Docking, unlinking and rotation](../docking-unlinking-rotation/) page says what a turn does to the belts docked there.
