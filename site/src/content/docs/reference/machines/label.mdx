A Label is a note on the board. You give it some text, a colour and a size, and it sits on the grid to say what the machines around it are for. It has no ports and takes no part in the flow: items never reach it and it never runs.

On the board below, a Label sits above a short line from a Source to a Sink, and the items on the line pass under it without touching it.

## Text, colour and size

Four fields set up a Label, and its panel in the app edits all four in one step, so one Undo puts them all back.

- <Fact id="config.Label.text" /> is the note itself, up to <Fact id="limit.max_label_bytes" />.
- <Fact id="config.Label.colour" /> picks the Label's colour from the list in its panel.
- <Fact id="config.Label.w" /> and <Fact id="config.Label.h" /> set its size in cells, up to <Fact id="limit.label_max_w" /> and <Fact id="limit.label_max_h" />. The Label in this example is set larger than the size it's placed at.

The troubleshooting entry for [a setting past a limit](../../../troubleshooting/refusals/#refusal-over-limit) says what to do when the app refuses a longer text or a larger size.

## On the grid

A Label covers the cells its size gives it, the same as a machine, so a machine, a belt or another Label can't go on those cells. When the app refuses a resize because something is in the way, the entry for [a resize that would cover something](../../../troubleshooting/refusals/#refusal-resize-covers) says how to make room. A Label never turns, and a belt or a wire can't connect to one; the entry for [a Label that doesn't connect](../../../troubleshooting/refusals/#refusal-label-does-not-connect) covers that refusal.
