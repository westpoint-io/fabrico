To buffer events behind a slow step, give them somewhere to wait: a long [belt](../../reference/files/glossary/#term-belt) in front of the slow part holds a burst of [items](../../reference/files/glossary/#term-item) in order until the slow part takes each one. It puts [backpressure](../../concepts/backpressure/) to work, the way a line packs up behind its slowest part.

The board below has a [Source](../../reference/files/glossary/#term-source), a long belt that is the buffer, a short belt at the slowest pace, a [Factory](../../reference/files/glossary/#term-factory) and a Debug [Sink](../../reference/files/glossary/#term-sink), left to right. The long belt's end is joined to the start of the short one, which feeds the Factory. Three bursts of 12 webhooks arrive 40 ticks apart.

## How the board works

Press Play and the board stops on these three [ticks](../../reference/files/glossary/#term-tick), the engine's steps:

1. Tick 64: The Source has put the first burst of 12 webhooks on the long belt, and they move along it in order toward the slow belt.
2. Tick 140: The Source has passed on all 36 webhooks from three bursts and holds none. The long belt is full, its items waiting in the order they came.
3. Tick 241: The Factory takes the first order off the slow belt. From here it gets one item every 20 ticks, and the rest wait in order on the belts.

The Source's backlog peaks at 11 waiting webhooks, on the tick after each burst arrives. By tick 600 the Debug Sink has logged orders 1 to 17, and the other 19 are still on the belts in order: 11 on the long belt, 7 on the slow belt and 1 on the belt to the Debug Sink.

## The Factory code

The Factory runs this code once for each item that reaches it:

```js title="Factory code"
// the work behind the slow step
export default function (input, ctx) {
  ctx.emit(0, { order: input.order, done: true });
}
```

A Factory has no pace of its own, so on this board the slow belt in front of it stands in for the slow step, such as a service that can only take so many calls. The buffer works the same whatever the code does.

## Trade-offs

- A belt holds <Fact id="limit.slots_per_tile" bare /> items a tile, so this eight-tile buffer holds 32, and a belt can be up to <Fact id="limit.max_belt_tiles" /> long. [Belts are queues](../../concepts/belts-are-queues/) says how a belt's length and pace set how long an item waits.
- A Source keeps up to <Fact id="limit.source_pending_cap" bare /> waiting webhooks, and past that it drops each new one with no item and no fault, so a buffer only helps up to there. The sender still gets 200 for a dropped webhook, so its own retries won't bring it back; [Webhook ingress](../../reference/code-and-http/webhook-ingress/#what-the-app-answers) lists what the app answers. This board sends bursts of 12, and its Source's backlog peaks at 11. Bursts that keep coming into a full line add up: once the belts are full, the backlog grows with each burst. [Limits](../../reference/board/limits/#queues) lists the caps.
- The slow step is a belt at <Fact id="limit.belt_pace_max" />, the slowest pace there is: in the inspector, set Belt pace to its Slowest end. Draw the long belt to end next to the slow belt's start and connect them with Join two belts ([Commands](../../reference/files/commands/) has the gesture), so items cross straight onto the slow belt. Set the pace before you join them, since the inspector treats joined belts as one run and Apply sets the pace on every belt in it. A slow belt docked straight onto a machine's output keeps its waiting items at its start, where the board doesn't draw them.
- Keep a Tee out of the line in front of the slow belt. A Tee doesn't wait for one busy branch while the other has room: the busy branch's copy becomes a <Fact id="fault.BranchBusy" /> fault instead of waiting in a buffer. [Send a webhook to multiple URLs](../send-to-multiple-urls/) shows a Tee.
